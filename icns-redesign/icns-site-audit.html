ICNS website audit

A full crawl and review of westernsydney.edu.au/icns, roughly 100 pages across seven sections. The research story is strong. The site undersells it: the flagship supercomputer is still announced in the future tense, the newest news item is from late 2023, two listing pages are empty, and nine distinct link targets return 404 from primary navigation paths. Everything below was verified against live HTTP responses and rendered pages on 24 August 2026.

P0The flagship is announced in the future tense

The homepage hero and the newest news item both say ICNS is going to build DeepSouth, the brain-scale supercomputer. That announcement is from December 2023 and the machine launched in April 2024. Two years on, the centre's single strongest asset reads as a stale press release, and it doubles as the homepage meta description, so the future tense is also what Google shows. DeepSouth should be a permanent facility page in present tense: what it is, what it runs, how researchers get access.

P0Sections that say "nothing is happening here"

  • Current Vacancies and Current Scholarships both render "No results were found". An empty page with no date is indistinguishable from an abandoned one. Either populate them, or replace with a short standing paragraph ("openings are advertised via ... register interest at ...").
  • Events stops at "2023 Events" and a 2023 NeuroEng Workshop, and the landing page has 34 words. In the top navigation of every page, "2023 Events" is a freshness stamp working against you.
  • News shows no dates on the listing, and the newest article is Dec 2023. Undated news hides staleness until a visitor clicks, then loses their trust when they notice.

P1Three overlapping content trees

The same material lives under /icns/research, /icns/research-projects, and /icns/resources, and the navigation lists "Research Videos" in two different sections. The videos actually resolve under three URL patterns (hyphen and underscore variants both return 200, plus the dead perception copies), which splits search ranking and makes the CMS harder to maintain. One "Research" tree covering streams, applications, projects, and outputs would remove most of the confusion and most of the broken links at their source.

P1Thin hub pages and slug cruft

  • Landing pages for Events (34 words), Partnership (43), Projects (67), Resources (75) exist only to hold links their parent nav already shows.
  • Publications, for a research centre, is a 131-word page pointing at individual Google Scholar profiles. A curated "selected publications" list by year, or an automated feed, is table stakes for credibility with academic and defence-industry visitors.
  • Visible CMS versioning in public URLs: reproducible-research3, publication-support-materials2, first-images-from-space!2.
  • People are buried at /icns/about/people/... and the homepage never links to them. For a research centre, people are a primary destination and belong in the top navigation.

P2Accessibility and polish

  • The homepage has no h1; its heading structure starts at duplicated h2 nav items.
  • Missing alt text: 7 of 10 homepage images, 14 of 14 researcher portraits, all 27 open-PhD-project thumbnails.
  • Card descriptions are truncated mid-sentence with "..." followed by "Read More" on the research and applications pages, so the scannable text carries half a thought.
  • Contact page office address is incomplete ("Level 2, Building BA, Great Western Highway" with no campus or suburb for the street address).
  • Images ship as PNG/JPEG only; no WebP/AVIF variants.

03 · Visual passWhat the pages look like

The live site is the standard light WSU template: white ground, crimson #990033 headings, dark text panels over the hero imagery. Verified against an extension-free render — an earlier pass through a browser running a dark-mode extension produced false findings (a "fixed dark theme" and an unreadable header) that are corrected here.

P1Homepage composition

  • The hero is a four-slide carousel with an underlined title and a "FIND OUT MORE..." button whose label trails off in an ellipsis. The slide imagery is a generic server-room render rather than a photo of DeepSouth itself, and only the first slide is ever seen by most visitors.
  • Section headings are underlined hyperlinks in the pattern "NEWS | Discover what's happening at ICNS", a 2010s portal idiom that makes every heading read as unvisited link text.
  • The welcome section leans on an embedded video thumbnail plus four bolded "We study / We design / We discover / We train" paragraphs; serviceable copy, but the page has no h1 above it.

P1Cards and imagery

  • Card summaries truncate mid-word: "at the scale of the human b...", "further cements IC...", "pioneerin...". Nine near-identical outline "READ MORE" buttons repeat down the page, none saying where they lead.
  • Thumbnail imagery has no system: the Master's Program card is a text poster cropped so it reads "ER OF / OMORPHIC / EERING", Open PhD Projects gets an abstract grey 3D render, and news cards mix real photography (Astrosite container, satellite imagery) with stock-style graphics. The real photos are the strongest images on the site; the decoration is the weakest.
  • Portraits on the people pages are black-and-white with inconsistent crops, presented in a single wide column of alternating photo/text rows.

P2Forced dark mode breaks the header

Under a dark-mode browser extension (a common accessibility setting), the page ground turns near-black while the header keeps WSU black #262223 for the centre's name and the logo's "UNIVERSITY" line — measured at 1.1:1 contrast, effectively invisible. Not a defect of the stock site, but worth knowing: a color-scheme declaration and a dark-safe header treatment would make the site degrade gracefully for those users.

04 · StrengthsWhat to keep and build on

  • The story inventory is enviable: DeepSouth, event cameras on the ISS, space imaging, the Intel partnership, a NASA internship, Astrosite. The raw material for a modern site already exists.
  • 24 open PhD projects, each with its own page, is a real recruitment engine; it needs metadata (stream, supervisor, status, date), not rework.
  • The research stream pages are properly written explanations, not brochure copy.
  • The template is responsive, loads fast, and the navigation labels are sensible. This is a content and information-architecture problem more than a technology one.

05 · StepsHow to correct it and make something good

Phase 1 — repair (CMS edits, roughly two days)

  • 1. Retarget the 9 dead links using the working URLs in the table above; delete the dead news item, archive-profile links, and the Vision page's impact link.
  • 2. Put DeepSouth in the present tense. Rewrite the hero slide and the news item as a standing facility page (what it is, what it runs, how to get access), and fix the homepage meta description.
  • 3. Clear the staleness signals. Dates on every news card; vacancies/scholarships shells replaced with two sentences of standing copy plus a contact; "2023 Events" renamed to an events archive or dropped from the nav; 2020 videos labelled as archive footage.
  • 4. Homepage quick pass. Give the page an h1, rename the "FIND OUT MORE..." buttons to say where they go, and fix the ellipsis labels.

Phase 2 — restructure (about a week)

  • 5. One Research tree. Merge /research, /research-projects and /resources into a single hierarchy (streams, applications, projects, publications, videos) with redirects from the old and underscore-duplicate URLs. This removes the class of bug that produced most of the 404s.
  • 6. Promote People to the top navigation and link them from the homepage; a research centre's people are a primary destination, not an About sub-sub-page.
  • 7. Rewrite card copy to fit. Every card gets a summary written to length (no mid-word "pioneerin..." truncation) and a labelled action ("View the program", not a ninth "READ MORE").
  • 8. Real publications page. Selected publications by year, curated or fed from ORCID/Scholar, replacing the 131-word pointer page.
  • 9. Accessibility sweep. Homepage h1, alt text on the 48+ images missing it (start with portraits and project thumbnails), untruncated link text.

Phase 3 — make it nice (design, two to four weeks)

  • 10. Choose a colourway and commit. Two defensible directions: keep the light WSU ground with all media (event-camera footage, star fields, figures) on dark plates, or take the whole site dark — neuromorphic imagery lives on black, and a dark centre site would stand out among WSU pages. Either way it needs a deliberate token set per the 2026 brand (white and crimson upweighted; on dark grounds use a brightened crimson, since raw #990033 fails contrast on black), and a color-scheme declaration so forced-dark extensions degrade gracefully.
  • 11. Replace the hero carousel with one static hero. A real photograph of DeepSouth or the Astrosite dome, one sentence, one CTA. Carousels hide three-quarters of their content and this one ships with a visible scrollbar.
  • 12. Fix the vertical rhythm. Consistent section spacing (one scale, roughly 64–96px steps) in place of the current full-viewport voids between sections.
  • 13. Build an imagery system from what ICNS already has. Event-camera output is visually unlike anything other centres can show: star trails, spike rasters, the foosball robot, ISS footage. Use real captures and lab photography as the only thumbnail sources; retire cropped posters and stock renders.
  • 14. One card system. Date, stream tag, and status on every news/project card, set in a consistent metadata row, so freshness and ownership are visible at a glance.
A note on ceilings. Items 1–5 are achievable inside the university CMS and will fix the credibility problems, which are the real modernisation blockers. If the ambition is a genuinely contemporary research-centre site (live publication feeds, project filtering, interactive demos of event-camera output), that argues for a separately built centre site on the ICNS design system, with the CMS pages kept as the university-facing shell that links to it.
