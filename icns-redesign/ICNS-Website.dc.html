Sensing · Computation · Deployed systems

Sensors and processors built on the principles of biological brains.

ICNS builds event-based sensors, spiking processors and the algorithms between them, then proves them in the field — in orbit, in bushland and on the bench at Western Sydney University. Partners come to us for hardware that works where conventional cameras and conventional computers do not.

Research

Three themes, one continuous pipeline from photon to decision.

The centre is organised around what happens to information: how it is captured, how it is computed on, and how the resulting instrument survives contact with the world. Each theme runs its own hardware and its own field programme, and every project crosses at least two.

01 · Capture

Perception

Event sensors report per-pixel brightness changes at microsecond resolution with over 120 dB of dynamic range. Alongside vision, the centre works in neuromorphic audio and tactile sensing.

Event-based vision sensors Cochlear and auditory models Tactile and proprioceptive arrays
02 · Compute

Processing

Spiking networks in dedicated hardware, and the algorithms that make sparse asynchronous data tractable. Deep South extends the same principles to brain-scale simulation.

Spiking neural network hardware Event-driven algorithms Brain-scale simulation
03 · Deploy

Sensing in the field

Instruments built to run unattended: mobile observatories tracking objects in orbit, cameras counting insects in moving vegetation, systems with no network and a milliwatt power budget.

Space situational awareness Environmental and ecological monitoring Autonomous and remote platforms
