For collaborators

What you can use, and how

Most people arrive here having seen the footage and wanting to know the practical part: whether they can get their hands on any of it, what it costs, and how long it takes. This page answers that as plainly as we can.

Instruments

What there is

Event cameras and the labAvailable now

Commercial and research event-based sensors, the recording rigs built around them, and the people who know how to get clean data out of them. This is the fastest way to find out whether the technology suits your problem, because the answer usually comes from one afternoon of recording rather than a study.

Bring the scene you care about. If it moves too fast, sits in awkward light, or has to be watched continuously on very little power, we can usually tell you inside a day whether an event sensor helps.

AstrositeAvailable, by arrangement

A mobile observatory in a shipping container: event-based telescopes that track satellites and debris in full daylight, where conventional optics saturate. It travels, so it can be deployed to a site rather than requiring you to come to Werrington.

Deployments are scheduled around existing commitments, so the useful first question is what you need observed and when, not how the instrument works.

DeepSouthIn build

The brain-scale spiking simulation machine is being built and is not yet in service. We are not taking access requests for it, and would rather say so than take your details and go quiet.

If large-scale spiking simulation is the reason you are here, tell us anyway and we will come back to you when it is running.

Software and dataOpen, no agreement needed

The centre's tools are published openly and need no permission, no agreement and no conversation. The camera simulator in particular lets you develop against an event sensor before you own one, which makes it the cheapest possible way to test an idea.

Engagement

Ways to work together

Ordered roughly by how much commitment each one asks for. Most partnerships that end up large started at the top of this list.

01

A conversation

Describe the measurement problem. We tell you whether neuromorphic sensing genuinely helps, including when it does not. That answer is free and is often the whole engagement.

Best when you are not yet sure this is your problem.

02

A student project

Sponsor or co-supervise a capstone, masters or doctoral project on your question. Cheap, low-risk, and you meet the people who might later join you.

Best first commitment for most companies.

03

A feasibility study

A short scoped piece of work with a defined question and a written answer at the end. Typically recording in your environment and reporting what the data supports.

Best when you need evidence for an internal decision.

04

Contract research

A funded programme against your requirement, with agreed milestones and deliverables.terms to confirm

Best when the direction is settled and the work is real.

05

A joint funding bid

Going after external funding together, where a partner contribution unlocks a considerably larger programme than either side would run alone.schemes to confirm

Best when the work is longer than one budget cycle.

06

Hardware and licensing

Prototype sensors, custom recording hardware, or licensing what the centre has already built.availability to confirm

Best when you need the device, not the study.

Getting started

How it actually begins

  1. Write to the centre

    One email. Say what you need to see, how fast it moves, what light it sits in, and what power and connectivity you have where it lives. Those four facts decide almost everything, and they are usually enough for a first answer.

  2. A scoping conversation

    Half an hour, no agreement in place, no charge. We will say plainly if a conventional camera would do the job, because sending you away with the right answer is worth more to us than a project that disappoints.

  3. An agreement, if one is needed

    Confidentiality first if you are bringing something commercially sensitive, then whatever the work itself requires.standard agreements and typical lead time to confirm

  4. Something small and real

    A recording session, a student project, a short study. Something that produces evidence in months rather than years, so both sides learn whether a bigger programme is worth it.
