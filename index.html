Perception

How do you see the world?

You might say: with your eyes.

But your eyes don't record the world. You respond to change. You move your attention. You discard almost everything you are given — and from those incomplete signals your brain builds a model good enough to act on.

So what happens if we build machines that meet the same problem the same way?

→ Neuromorphic perception

Cognition

How do you remember it?

Probably not by keeping a perfect reconstruction of everything you have ever seen.

You remember what was there, where it was, what it was near, and roughly when you saw it. You know where the door is without looking at it. That is a cognitive map — and it is the thing a robot with a perfect point cloud still does not have.

So we build the memory the same way: bind what to where, and query it later.

→ Cognitive maps · VSA memory

Perception & Cognition — the work is the part in between: turning what changed into what you know.

In practice that means translating chaotic event streams into resilient spatial awareness — bridging neuromorphic perception with symbolic reasoning, for autonomous systems that sense less to understand more.
